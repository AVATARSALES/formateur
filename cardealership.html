Discussion Questions — Model Answers (BTS / BUT)

Q1. Ousmane grants €3,000 in under 2 minutes. What more would you have asked for as Ms Loto?
Model answer points: Go further: extra discount, free accessories, servicing or warranty. A salesperson who gives way quickly reveals a large margin; the buyer concludes there is still more to get.
Q2. "Very competitive price compared to other brands": value argument or positioning argument?
Model answer points: It is a positioning argument (it talks about the market). A value argument talks about the customer: "This model saves you €80 a month and protects your children."
Q3. Write a sentence that works "your children" into the presentation of the boot.
Model answer points: "With 520 litres of boot space, you load your children's school bags, the sports bag and the pushchair without folding the seats down."
Q4. What could Ousmane observe during the test drive?
Model answer points: Ms Loto's spontaneous reactions: comfort, quietness, visibility, ease of manoeuvring, glances to the rear seats. These are buying signals to rephrase on return ("You seemed to appreciate the visibility…").

Problem (frustrations), Implication (consequences) and Need-Payoff (projection of benefits) are missing.S: "How many trips do you make per week?" · P: "What bothers you about your current car?" · I: "When it's full, how do you manage school bags and shopping?" · N: "If you saved €80 a month on fuel, what would you do with that budget?"
3. FAB argumentationHe states features ("5L/100km", "150 horsepower") and stops there: no advantage, no customer benefit.F: 5L/100km combined cycle · A: lower consumption at every fill-up · B: "around €80 saved per month on your daily trips — a budget for your children's activities".
4. Use of the customer's vocabularyMs Loto says "family", "children", "school activities", "shopping". Ousmane answers with his words ("generous space", "large boot"): he does not pick up her key words."For your shopping and your children's things for their school activities, the 520 L boot means you don't have to fold the seats down."
5. Handling the price objectionImmediate 9.5% concession with no resistance, value argument or return. Effect: the price loses credibility and the dealership's margin is eroded.Acknowledge ("I understand") → Question ("What matters to you beyond price?") → Argue the value (safety, fuel savings, space) → Concession in return (financing, trade-in, accessories).
6. Negotiation and concessions in return€3,000 discount granted without asking for anything: no trade-in, no in-house financing, no accessories. Value left on the table: at least €3,000 of direct margin, plus ancillary margins not captured.Examples: 1) in-house financing; 2) trade-in of the old vehicle; 3) accessories pack (mats, roof bars); or an extended warranty / maintenance contract.
7. Making the most of the test driveTest drive mentioned but not exploited; on return: "What did you think?" — a closed question that opens nothing. Spontaneous reactions are neither observed nor rephrased."What did you like most during the drive?" · "How did you find the visibility and comfort for your children?" · "Can you picture doing your daily trips in it?"

No closed alternative, no benefits summary, no projection into everyday use.Summary + closed alternative: "You liked the space, the safety and the fuel savings. Would you prefer financing over 48 months or paying cash? Delivery at the end of this month or the start of next?"
9. Soft skills and sales stanceActive listening partial (no rephrasing), empathy without firmness on price, communication adapted but too technical. Dominant soft skill: empathy / service mindset. Missing soft skill: critical thinking and commercial firmness (results orientation).Empathy without firmness leads to a quick concession: the customer gets €3,000 with no effort and the salesperson loses credibility and margin. A balanced salesperson listens, rephrases, then holds the price in exchange for concessions.

Discussion Questions — Model Answers (Master's)

Q1. Write one Implication and one Need-Payoff question.
Model answer points: Implication: "If your current car has no Isofix, what impact does that have on your children's safety day to day?" · Need-Payoff: "If you saved €80 a month on fuel, how would you use that budget?"
Q2. Calculate the margin lost with 9.5% and nothing in return, then what financing + accessories would allow.
Model answer points: Margin lost: €3,000 outright. With in-house financing (€800–1,200 margin) + accessories pack (€400–600): €1,200 to €1,800 of additional margin. With the discount brought down to ~€1,500, the net impact is close to zero or even positive, and the customer feels she has negotiated.
Q3. "I'm ready to negotiate a little" → "What price did you have in mind?" What alternative question?
Model answer points: "What is important to you beyond price?" (financing, trade-in, equipment). The salesperson keeps the initiative and opens the negotiation on overall value, not just price.
Q4. What are the 3 priority coaching points for Ousmane?
Model answer points: 1) Skills: deepen discovery (full SPIN). 2) Stance: firmness on price, no giving way without arguing the value. 3) Process: systematic concessions in return for every discount, and exploiting the test drive.
