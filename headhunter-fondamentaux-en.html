Scenario summary
Learners are recruiters at SPEED-UP, a hyper-growth start-up (Series A raised, “organised chaos”). The sales team must grow from 2 to 10 people in 3 months. The CEO changes strategy every week. No structured CRM. The team has to “bring in revenue” immediately.

Mission: review 4 candidate files for the Senior Business Developer position (pure hunting), read their simulated interview dialogues, and choose the profile best suited to this chaotic start-up culture.
SPEED-UP context
Company: Start-up, Series A, hyper-growth · Culture: Organised chaos, agility, immediate results · Position: Senior Business Developer, pure hunting · Constraint: 2→10 sales reps in 3 months, empty CRM, outdated product sheets
The 4 candidate profiles
Ousmane
“The Architect”
Top 5 School · 5 years at a major bank
Wants to document every change before acting (“a 50-page guide”)
Refuses to prospect without an updated CRM
Stops a demo when a bug appears and waits for an official fix
“Quality comes before speed”
❌ Unsuitable — Too rigid for start-up chaos. Would waste 3 weeks on an audit before the first call.
Béatrice
“The Pirate”
Top 5 School · 5 years Freelance + Digital agency
Scrapes competitors' LinkedIn + aggressive cold mailing from D+1
Bypasses the switchboard, logs everything in a personal Excel file
Accepts the GDPR risk: “better to ask for forgiveness”
“My team is my results” — maximum sales intensity
✅ Recommended profile — Immediate sales intensity, total autonomy, guaranteed results from D+1. With GDPR supervision, she is the SPEED-UP profile par excellence.
Idriss
“The Mediator”
Top 5 School · 5 years at an international NGO
Cohesion first: team lunches, “how do you feel” meetings
Ready to “slow down the pace” to protect the team
Would like to review the CEO's targets to “readjust expectations”
“A happy team is a high-performing team”
❌ Unsuitable — A management/HR profile misplaced in a hunting Business Dev role. Would sacrifice targets for harmony.
Diane
“The Hunter”
Top 5 School · 5 years in photocopier sales (one-shot)
Experience in consultative selling of a complex product on a short cycle
Used to field work without marketing support
Measurable results + autonomy + managing her pipeline solo
Adapts her pitch to each type of decision-maker
🔵 Solid alternative — Excellent field profile with no legal risk. Defensible if the priority is safety over maximum performance.
Teaching key
The scenario's trap: all 4 candidates have the same basic CV (Top 5 School + 5 years of experience). The difference lies in their behaviour in the face of chaos, not in their degrees.

Ousmane = process before results → unsuitable · Idriss = harmony before performance → management profile in the wrong role · Diane = autonomy + field + zero risk → solid alternative · Béatrice = maximum sales intensity + total autonomy → recommended profile for SPEED-UP with GDPR supervision.
🔥 Trainer debate — Diane vs Béatrice
🔵 Arguments for Diane — Solid alternative
→ Field + short cycle + solo = direct transfer to B2B hunting
→ Zero GDPR risk, zero team conflict
→ Proven autonomy without marketing support
→ Minimal managerial supervision is enough
→ Valid alternative if: SPEED-UP puts legal safety ahead of maximum performance
✅ Arguments for Béatrice — Recommended profile
→ Outstanding immediate sales intensity
→ “Pirate” culture perfectly aligned with an aggressive start-up DNA
→ Cold mailing + scraping = full pipeline from D+1
→ Total autonomy: no need for support, CRM or processes
→ Profile chosen: aggressive growth + CEO ready to supervise GDPR = ideal SPEED-UP profile
💬 Key message for learners
In real-life recruitment, there is no universal right answer — there is the right answer for this specific context and these specific priorities. Béatrice is the recommended profile for SPEED-UP because her sales intensity matches the chaotic start-up DNA — provided there is GDPR supervision. Diane is a solid alternative if legal safety comes first. This module trains learners to argue a behaviour-based choice, not to recite a single answer.
Activity
In teams of 3-4, learners read the 4 application files (CV + simulated interview dialogues), analyse each candidate's behaviours and soft skills in concrete situations (chaos, product bug, team conflict), then deliberate and defend their choice in front of the other teams. The trainer leads a comparative debrief on the soft skills expected in a hyper-growth start-up.

Recommended session plan (35 – 40 min)
1
0 – 5 min
Recruiter briefing
Introduce SPEED-UP: “Chaotic start-up, revenue right now, 8 strategy changes in 3 months. You are hiring your future hunter.” Do not give any clues about the right answer. Hand out the 4 files.
2
5 – 23 min
Individual module + team deliberation
Reading of the 4 dialogues (7 min solo), then team deliberation (11 min). Each team prepares its recommendation with 3 precise behavioural arguments taken from the dialogues.
3
23 – 33 min
Inter-team presentations
Each team presents its choice in 2 min. The other teams may challenge it. The trainer notes the behavioural arguments without revealing the “right” answer yet.
4
33 – 45 min
Debrief — Reveal the grid AND the Diane/Béatrice debate
Reveal the reading grid (behaviours vs culture). Stress the Béatrice paradox (effective but risky, under conditions). Key step: if your debrief concludes on Béatrice, spell out the 2 criteria that make her defensible — growth urgency > legal risk AND planned managerial supervision. Tell learners that “Diane with no risk” and “Béatrice under conditions” are the 2 defensible answers, the other 2 being ruled out. Run the quiz.
Trainer tips
🎲
Managing the Diane vs Béatrice debate. Béatrice is often chosen by “results-oriented” learners AND may be the pick in some video debriefs. Do not dismiss it too quickly — let both camps argue, then ask the decisive question: “Does SPEED-UP have a manager available to supervise Béatrice on GDPR every day?” If not → Diane. If yes → both are defensible. The nuance is the real lesson of the workshop.
⚖️
Consistency with the video debrief. If your video debrief concludes on Béatrice, explain the criteria used to learners: aggressive growth priority + strong managerial supervision planned. Make clear that Diane remains the “no-risk” answer and Béatrice the “high performance under conditions” answer. Both are valid — what matters is the behavioural argumentation.
💡
The Idriss paradox. His values are positive in absolute terms (cohesion, listening) but fatal in this context. Show that the same qualities can be strengths or obstacles depending on the company culture.

1 — Participant introduction
To project or read aloud before starting

In this module, you will take on the role of recruiter for a hyper-growth start-up. Your mission: analyse 4 candidate files, spot the key behavioural signals and select the Senior Business Developer best suited to the company culture.


Client context — SPEED-UP, a start-up in its hyper-growth phase (Series A raised)

  • Situation: The sales team must grow from 2 to 10 people in 3 months.
  • Culture: “Organised chaos”. The CEO changes strategy every Tuesday.
  • Tools: No structured CRM, no up-to-date product sheets.
  • Top priority: Bring in revenue right now — no matter how.

You have 4 minutes to read the files, then 10 minutes to deliberate as a team. You will then have to defend your choice with 3 behavioural arguments taken from the dialogues — not from the CV.


⚠️ Reminder: the 4 candidates have exactly the same CV (Top 5 School + 5 years of experience). The difference is 100% behavioural.

3 — SPEED-UP employer branding elements
✅ What SPEED-UP offers
→ Hyper-growth = fast career progression (BizDev → Team Lead in 6 months with results)
→ Total autonomy: no micro-management, no daily reporting
→ Direct, visible impact: your deals keep the company alive
→ Uncapped variable pay (no commission cap)
→ “Series A” environment: stock options possible
⚠️ What SPEED-UP demands
→ Perform without a safety net: empty CRM, outdated product sheets
→ Adapt to a shifting strategy (CEO = every Tuesday)
→ Generate your own leads without marketing support
→ High tolerance for risk and ambiguity
→ Measurable results expected from week 2
🎯 Expected cultural profile
→ “Move fast and learn”: mistakes are allowed, slowness is not
→ Solo player able to coach juniors later on
→ Entrepreneurial mindset: creates processes rather than waiting for them
→ Resilience: a “no” = the next opportunity
⚡ Recruitment red flags
→ “I need a complete CRM to get started” → Ousmane = 🚫
→ “I first want to understand the long-term strategy” → Ousmane/Idriss = 🚫
→ “I'm waiting for the product sheets before prospecting” → eliminatory
→ “I'll handle it my own way” → Béatrice = ✅ if supervision is planned
