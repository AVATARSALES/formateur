Scenario summary
You: Department manager, 25 years old · Marc: 45-year-old team member, 10 years' seniority, a pillar of the department — arrives at 9:20 instead of 9:00, for the 3rd time this week. The team is starting to comment.

Goal: Stop the lateness without breaking the motivation of this expert, using the DESC method.
The DESC method
🔵 DESC — NVC tool for caring reprimands
D
Describe — observable, quantified facts, no judgement. "Monday 9:30, Tuesday 9:15, this morning 9:20."
E
Express — the concrete impact on the team and the service. "Sophie finds herself alone facing the customer flow."
S
Specify — the co-built solution, not an imposed one. "On your side, what solution can you put in place?"
C
Conclude — the commitment and the framework of trust. "Verbal commitment. If it's sorted by Monday, no need to go further."
The 11 key steps
1 — Invitation
Take Marc aside without humiliating him in front of the team
✓ "Could you pop into my office for five minutes?" (discretion)❌ "Marc, my office. Right now!" (public humiliation)❌ "Did your alarm clock fail?" (sarcasm in front of colleagues)
2 — The Facts
Describe quantified facts, not judgements (NVC)
✓ "Monday 9:30, Tuesday 9:15, this morning 9:20. Is that right?" (precise facts + confirmation)❌ "You've been late all week." (generalisation = defensiveness)❌ "You lack professionalism." (judgement = shutdown)
3 — Minimisation
Marc tries to downplay the seriousness — hold firm with empathy
✓ "I hear your difficulties. However, over three days that adds up to 45 minutes of unplanned absence." (acknowledge + quantify)❌ "I know all about transport excuses." (denial of emotion)❌ "Try to make an effort, it's just for the example." (vague + condescending)
4 — The Impact
Explain WHY it's a problem (not for yourself — for the team)
✓ "When you're not here at 9:00, Sophie finds herself alone facing the flow. It puts her in difficulty and disrupts shelf stocking." (team impact)❌ "It puts me in an awkward position with the director." (personal impact = self-centred)❌ "If this continues: warning, suspension." (premature threat)
5 — Seniority
Marc plays the 10-years card — defuse without minimising
✓ "I'm not questioning the quality of your work or your commitment, which are exemplary. We're only talking about punctuality in the morning." (separating the two issues)❌ "Seniority doesn't give you every right." (unnecessary harshness)❌ "True, you're a pillar... but mornings are complicated." (vague)
6 — Root cause
Look for the hidden problem through empathy
✓ "Marc, this isn't like you. Has there been a recent personal change?" (open door)❌ "Are you having sleep problems? Lifestyle issues?" (intrusive + paternalistic)❌ "Whatever the reason, your reasons stay at the door." (total shutdown)
7 — Solution
Co-build — Marc reveals: he drops the children at school (his wife changed her hours)
✓ "From your point of view, what solution can you put in place to reconcile this with 9:00?" (Marc suggests: before-school care at 8:30)❌ "Put them in before-school care or ask your wife to sort it out." (imposes without co-building)❌ "I'm changing your hours to 9:30–7:30." (unilateral decision → Marc: "I have my sports club!")
8 — Commitment
Formalise the precise timing of the agreement
✓ "That solution seems suitable. From when do you think you can make it work reliably?" (Marc: "It'll be sorted by Monday")❌ "I hope you'll keep your word. I'll be monitoring your hours to the minute." (surveillance = distrust)❌ "Ok great, try to do that soon." (vague, no commitment)
9 — Formalisation
Framework of trust — don't over-formalise if the agreement is sincere
✓ "Noted, Marc. A verbal commitment of trust between us. I don't need to go further if the situation is back to normal on Monday." (Marc: "We work on our word")❌ "I'm sending you a summary email — standard HR procedure." (Marc: "Is that really necessary?")❌ "I'm having you sign this formal warning." (Marc: "I refuse to sign that!")
10 — Closing
End by valuing the relationship and trust
✓ "Thank you for this frank discussion, Marc. I know I can count on your professionalism going forward. Have a good day on the floor."❌ "That's all. You can go back to work." (cold, no connection)❌ "Don't let me see you at 9:20 again, or there'll be trouble." (final threat)
11 — Follow-up
The following Monday: Marc arrives at 8:55 — acknowledge the effort publicly
✓ "Morning Marc! Good to see you in position on time. Thanks for keeping your commitment, it's appreciated." (positive reinforcement)❌ "8:55. Good. Keep it up, I'm watching." (Marc: "Always a pleasure to be spied on after 10 years")
3 possible endings
🏆
Ending 1 — Agreement + trust restored
Marc commits verbally, finds the solution himself (before-school care at 8:30), arrives at 8:55 on Monday. Manager/employee relationship strengthened.
🟡
Ending 2 — Vague agreement / lukewarm commitment
Marc says "I'll see what I can do, but I'm not promising anything." Imposed solution or over-heavy formalisation. Atmosphere deteriorates.
❌
Ending 3 — Open conflict / breakdown
Marc refuses to sign the warning or leaves the office angry. "This is deeply unfair." The situation has worsened.
Activity completed
The learner plays a young manager who must reprimand a senior team member for repeated lateness, using the DESC method. At each step the learner chooses the wording: discreet invitation, description of the facts (NVC), handling of minimisation, expressing the impact on the team, defusing the seniority card, finding the root cause, co-building a solution, commitment and follow-up.
Learning objectives
Knowledge
Define the 4 steps of the DESC method. Distinguish observable fact from judgement (Nonviolent Communication). Name the pitfalls of managerial reprimands (humiliation, threats, imposed solutions).

Skills
Describe a behavioural problem using figures and facts, without judgement. Take an employee aside for a talk without public humiliation. Identify a root cause with an open, empathetic question. Co-build a solution with the employee rather than imposing it. Formulate a precise commitment (who does what, by when).

Attitude
Adopt a posture that is both caring and firm. Show empathy for a personal constraint without losing sight of the objective. Value seniority while keeping the framework non-negotiable.

Quiz with answers — DESC method (5 questions)
1
MCQ
At the facts step, Marc says "I've had a few transport problems this week." What is the best response?
▶
✓"I hear your difficulties. However, over three days that adds up to 45 minutes of unplanned absence." — acknowledge + quantify without excusing
✗"I know all about transport excuses. At 9:00 you're here, full stop." — denial + authoritarian tone
✗"I understand, transport is hell. Try to make an effort." — capitulation without a framework
💡 NVC requires acknowledging the emotion BEFORE maintaining the framework. "I hear" + "However" = the magic sequence of a caring reprimand. Neither denial of the emotion nor abandonment of the objective.
2
MCQ
Marc says: "I've been here 10 years, I know my job, I stay late in the evening when needed!" How do you respond?
▶
✓"I'm absolutely not questioning the quality of your work or your commitment, which are exemplary. We're only talking about punctuality in the morning, which is a separate criterion." — separate seniority from the topic of the day
✗"Seniority doesn't give you every right. The director arrives on time." — true but harsh
✗"You're right, you're a pillar. It's just that mornings are a bit complicated." — vague + settles nothing
💡 The seniority card is a classic trap: either you deny it (breakdown) or you let it win (capitulation). The right answer acknowledges Marc's REAL commitment WHILE maintaining that the topics are separate. Marc reacts: "I was afraid you'd question my 10 years over a few minutes."
3
Matching
Match each DESC letter with its definition and the example from the scenario.
▶
D — Describe→Quantified facts without judgement: "Monday 9:30, Tuesday 9:15, this morning 9:20 — 45 min in total"
E — Express→Impact on the team: "Sophie finds herself alone facing the customer flow at opening"
S — Specify→Co-built solution: "From your point of view, what solution can you put in place?"
C — Conclude→Verbal commitment of trust: "Sorted by Monday — no need to go further"
💡 The S is the step most often missed: managers impose a solution instead of letting the employee find it. Marc himself proposed before-school care at 8:30 — a solution he owns and will stick to.
4
True / False
When an employee voluntarily proposes a reliable solution, sending a summary email as "standard HR procedure" is good practice.
▶
✓FALSE
💡 Marc reacts: "Is it really necessary to do paperwork for this?" Over-formalisation destroys the trust built during the discussion. A sincere verbal commitment from a 10-year employee is worth more than an email. Written formalisation is reserved for situations without agreement or for repeat offences.
5
MCQ
On Monday morning, Marc arrives at 8:55. What is the manager's best reaction?
▶
✓"Morning Marc! Good to see you in position on time. Thanks for keeping your commitment, it's appreciated." — immediate positive reinforcement
✗"8:55. Good. Keep it up, I'm watching." — Marc: "Always a pleasure to be spied on after 10 years"
✗Say nothing, punctuality is the norm (no need to acknowledge it)
💡 Positive follow-up is as important as the reprimand. Positively reinforced behaviour is repeated. Marc unconsciously expects recognition of his effort — giving it closes the process and consolidates the relationship. Ignoring it sends the message "being on time is normal" without acknowledging the effort of reorganising his family life.
Recommended session plan (35 min)
1
0 – 5 min
DESC in 2 minutes flat
Present the 4 letters without examples. Ask each learner to write one sentence per letter for the Marc scenario before playing. Compare after the module.
2
5 – 20 min
Individual module (15 min)
Full game. Learners note the 2 most difficult steps. Trainer notes the most frequent errors (usually: step 5 seniority and step 7 co-building).
3
20 – 30 min
DESC debrief + role-play
In pairs: A = manager, B = Marc (hard version: plays the seniority card AND minimises). Goal: come away with a precise commitment from Marc in under 7 minutes.
4
30 – 40 min
Quiz + NVC summary
Individual quiz. Summary: the difference between "you're late" (judgement) and "Monday 9:30, Tuesday 9:15, Wednesday 9:20" (fact) is the foundation of the whole method.
Trainer tips
🎯
Have learners write the facts before the module. "Describe Marc's behaviour in 20 words maximum, without any adjective." Impossible on the first try — reveals how automatic judgement is compared with observable fact.
💡
Insist on the "S" as the key step. Co-building is not managerial weakness — it is what guarantees buy-in. Marc found the before-school care himself: he cannot say "this was imposed on me".
🔗
Transfer to other field situations. DESC applies to: lateness, inappropriate behaviour at the till, conflict between colleagues, resistance to a planogram change. One tool, a thousand situations.
